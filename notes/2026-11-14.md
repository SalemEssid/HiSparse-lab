## Interpretation

1. **G4: Go by the pre-registered rule.** A split learned on other prompts removes 23% of PCIe page transfers at the same GPU memory (k = 128), keeping 83% of the best possible gain. The split depends on the model, not the prompt: folds agree at r = 0.99 (H2 supported, H3 supported).
2. **The split is not a simple depth profile.** Both fixed shapes are worse than uniform. The pyramid shape (lossy KV-eviction work gives early layers more) is badly wrong here: early layers are the most local. What matters is each layer's own miss curve.
3. **The threat to validity from dense traces does not change the result.** With sparse attention on, locality, per-layer profile and allocation are nearly the same, and an allocation learned on dense traces still removes 21% of misses on the sparse ones.
4. **Tails improve less than means.** UCP lowers mean misses per step by 26% and p99 by 14%; the worst step (687 pages) is the same in every scheme. *Hypothesis:* those bursts are cold misses, when the model starts reading a new part of the prompt, and no cache size can avoid them. For latency, the tail matters.
5. **Reading the pages already in the cache recovers about half of sparse attention's quality loss, at zero extra transfer.** KL −45% at k = 128, perplexity 1.395 → 1.280, top-1 agreement 91.9% → 93.7%.
6. **No trade-off between traffic and quality.** The miss-curve allocation also gives the best quality among the Quest configurations (KL 0.099 vs 0.102 uniform), while moving 23% fewer pages. *Hypothesis:* the layers whose selections change most, which UCP rewards, are also those where Quest's choice is least stable, so extra resident pages help most there.
7. **Most of Quest's quality loss is its estimate, not the budget.** With the same 128 pages chosen from the true attention, KL falls by 82%. Coverage alone does not predict quality: resident reading (92.0% coverage, 243 pages) has KL 0.102, oracle (92.7%, 128 pages) 0.033. Which pages are missing matters, not only how much mass.
8. **Caveats.** Misses are rare in this regime (≈ 1.4% at B = 2k): the 23% cut saves little time at batch 1. Quality is measured as divergence from full attention on text the full model wrote, not task accuracy. One model, seven prompts. Novelty of points 5 and 6 is unknown until a literature check.

## Candidate contributions

1. Per-layer GPU cache sizing from miss curves for hierarchical sparse KV: −23% PCIe traffic at equal memory, learned offline, transfers across prompts and from dense to sparse decoding.
2. Resident-page attention: attend to every page already in the GPU cache, not only the top-k. About half of the quality loss recovered for free.
3. The two combine without conflict: cache sizing chosen for traffic also improves quality.
