# 2026-11-13 · Day 4 · Phase 2 (Traces) → G2

G2: Quest coverage vs exact top-k measured.

## What was built

- `trace/quest.py`: Quest page summaries (incremental min/max per 16-token page), per-head upper bounds with GQA mapping, one shared ranking per layer (sink page, newest page, then head-averaged scores), and the coverage math.
- `trace/collect.py`: dense decoding with a logging attention function registered through transformers' `AttentionInterface`. Logging never changes the computation (tested). It writes one `.npz` per request with the **top 256 pages per step and layer in rank order**, so any k ≤ 256 is a prefix.
- Coverage is sampled every 50 steps at all 36 layers. Five rules are compared at k = 32, 64, 128, 256 pages: Quest shared (mean logit, the logged one), Quest shared (mean softmax), Quest per head, best shared pages (oracle), exact top-(16k) tokens per head.
- Design choices (assumptions): Workload A uses LongBench v2 with the 0-shot prompt, middle-truncated to 32,000 tokens, in **Qwen3 thinking mode** so decode runs ~1,000 natural steps (HiSparse's GLM-5.1 trace is also reasoning output). Sampling uses Qwen3's thinking settings (T = 0.6, top-k 20, top-p 0.95) with a fixed seed, because greedy decoding in thinking mode tends to loop, which would inflate locality.

## Interpretation

1. **Quest is a good enough selector for this study.** At k = 128 it captures 92.9% of the attention mass. The loss splits into two parts. Going from exact tokens to pages shared by all heads costs 3.5 points (98.1 → 94.6). Quest's estimate of which pages are best costs 1.7 more (94.6 → 92.9).
2. **Sharing one selection across heads costs nothing here, which was a surprise.** *Hypothesis:* the upper bound is a noisy estimate for any single head, and averaging 32 heads cancels much of that noise while the heads largely agree on which pages matter. Consequence: HiSparse's one-cache-per-layer interface loses no selection quality, and `mean_logit` stays the aggregation.
3. **Layers 0–2 are the weak spot (75–82%).** Their attention is spread broadly. *To verify:* the Quest authors also keep the first two layers dense for this reason. Phase 3 should simulate all 36 layers but report layers 0–2 separately.
4. **Locality is high, and higher than in HiSparse's trace.** 88.9% overlap means a cache holding only the last selection (B = k) would miss about 11% of pages. HiSparse measured 30% at B = k. This fits the operating-point concern: our k / context ratio is 6.4% against HiSparse's 2.0%. LRU at B = 2k will miss even less, which leaves less absolute room for allocation gains. Simulating k = 32, which matches HiSparse's ratio, is free from this trace because the top 256 were logged.
5. **Early signal for H1.** The B = k miss rate per layer ranges from 4.9% (layer 3) to 21.1% (layer 33), a 4× spread, with the worst locality in late layers. That is encouraging, but the decision depends on the slope of the miss curves near B = 2k and on the cold-miss share, which Phase 3 measures.
6. **Prompt quality.** Item 0 kept only 11% of its 284K-token context. The remaining Workload A traces should use items whose context fits with little truncation.

## Quality check: does Quest's selection loss hurt the model?

Question raised by Salem: the ~7% of attention Quest leaves out could change the model's output and spread through layers and steps. `trace/sparse_check.py` runs true sparse attention (each layer reads only its k selected pages at decode; prefill full) and compares it with full attention: passphrase at 5 depths of a 32K text, and teacher-forced next-token comparison on the 7 Workload A traces (400 steps each). Configurations: full; k = 128 and k = 32, each with all layers sparse and with layers 0–1 kept full. This does not affect the research question (cache size never changes outputs); it tests how realistic the traces are.

### Interpretation

1. **Retrieval survives, but the output distribution changes noticeably.** The passphrase is found at every depth even at k = 32. Yet at k = 128 the sparse model's most likely next token differs from the full model's at 8% of steps, and in 16% of steps the distributions differ by KL > 0.1. Salem's concern holds for training-free Quest on Qwen3-4B.
2. **The perplexity numbers exaggerate the loss.** The text was generated by the full model itself (sampled at T = 0.6), so the full model is near-certain about it (perplexity 1.155) and any change looks worse. In absolute terms, the probability given to the actual next token falls on average from e^−0.144 ≈ 0.87 to e^−0.333 ≈ 0.72 at k = 128. This is divergence from the full model, not task accuracy.
3. **The early layers are not the main source.** Keeping layers 0–1 full removes only 18% of the KL at k = 128.
4. **For the research question: no effect,** since cache size never changes outputs.
5. **For trace realism: the known threat (section 6.4) is real.** With 8% of greedy tokens differing, a truly sparse model would leave the full model's path within a few dozen tokens, and from then on select differently. Whether its locality differs is untested. The direct check: collect traces with sparse attention switched on and compare miss curves.

### Interpretation

1. **The simulator is validated.** Same policy order as HiSparse at both k, and the fast curves are exact.
2. **Misses are rare in our regime, and the ratio of k to context does not explain it.** LRU at 2k misses 1.4% of selected pages against HiSparse's 13.4%. k = 32, which matches HiSparse's ratio, gives the same rates as k = 128 (10.6% vs 10.9% at B = k, 1.5% vs 1.4% at 2k). This **falsifies the operating-point explanation** proposed on Nov 11 (and drawn in the G2 explainer). The remaining candidates: page-level selection is more stable than HiSparse's token-level selection (open question Q6), the model, the selector, or the workload.
3. **Reuse is short-range.** Going from B = k to B = 2k removes 87% of misses here (10.9% → 1.4%), against 55% in HiSparse (30% → 13.4%): pages that leave the selection usually come back soon.
4. **Q1 answered in practice.** HiSparse-LRU differs from plain LRU by at most 1.4% in any layer, so the one-pass LRU curves are a valid stand-in for allocation, even though the inclusion property is not exact for HiSparse's variant.
5. **H1 supported.** Layer miss rates span 0.2%–5.3% (26×), and the gain from 25% more cache spans 0.0–1.1 points: the slopes differ, not only the levels. The worst layers (33, 20, 29, 13, 15) match the Phase 2 overlap ranking.
6. **Early kill test passed.** The best possible split removes 25–34% of misses at k = 128, far above the 10% Go threshold. About half the misses are cold, so this is more than half of all removable misses. The decision now rests on H2: does a split learned on other prompts keep most of it? (Phase 4.)
7. **Large relative cut, small absolute traffic.** At ≈ 1.4% misses, one request moves about 64 pages (≈ 4 MiB) per decode step: about 0.2 ms at link speed against a ~31 ms step. Per-layer allocation would matter in practice only at large batch, at longer contexts, or with smaller caches. Any write-up must state this.
8. **The one-time fill is 6–11% of misses** and no allocation can remove it. Phase 4 should also report steady-state misses (after step 0); a warmup from prefill selections (ESS's LRU-Warmup) would remove these in deployment.

## Phase 4 started early (G4) and two follow-ups

Built `sim/run_g4.py` (leave-one-prompt-out UCP against uniform, pyramid, inverted, in-sample UCP and oracle; total, steady-state and per-step tail misses; HiSparse-LRU re-simulation), `--sparse-k` in `trace/collect.py` (traces from true sparse decoding, named `S128_`), and resident / oracle modes in `trace/sparse_check.py`. `run_phase4.sh` runs everything. All tests pass, including a step-by-step check that the resident-page set equals the LRU cache of `sim/cache.py`.

**New idea (Salem's quality concern):** the GPU cache holds B = 2k pages but attention reads only k. Reading every resident page costs no extra transfer and may win back quality, which would make per-layer cache size a joint knob for traffic and quality. Novelty: unknown, needs a literature check.
